# VeriFYD Universal Handle Registry — Handle Registration, Ownership and Dispute Policy

**Version:** 0.1 — DRAFT; NOT YET ACCEPTED OR ENFORCED  
**Prepared:** 15 November 2026  
**Proposed operator:** VeriFYD [CONFIRM LEGAL ENTITY, JURISDICTION AND REGISTERED CONTACT]  
**Status:** Consultation and legal review only. This draft creates no present user obligation, new fee, transfer right, reallocation, or binding promise. Existing published terms and application behavior remain unchanged until an approved version is lawfully adopted.

## 1. Scope and key distinction

This Policy governs proposed exclusive use of registered **VeriFYD handles within VeriFYD's own namespace**, including approved partner integrations that contractually recognize that namespace. It does not govern telephone numbers, domain registrations, social-media names, carrier assignments, or identities controlled by unrelated services.

**Registration is not legal title in a word or string.** The proposed entitlement is a continuing, conditional right to use an allocated handle inside the VeriFYD service under fair, published rules. A handle's uniqueness within VeriFYD does not prevent another entity from using that word elsewhere.

A **verified email** proves the registrant controlled a mailbox at a verification moment; it does **not** prove legal name, government identity, business formation, trademark ownership, professional licensure, or control of social accounts.

## 2. Registration and naming rules

- Each active account has one primary registered handle until a separately designed alias feature is approved.
- Current implementation accepts a handle of 3–24 characters, beginning with an ASCII letter, followed by ASCII letters, digits or underscores. Matching is case-insensitive.
- The registry stores the original preferred display spelling and a lowercase uniqueness key. Changing capitalization does not create a second registration.
- A valid current email verification flow is required for an initial claim. Existing safeguards permit one current VeriFYD identity per verified email, subject to separate account/recovery processes.
- Current protected operational names include administrator, security, support and VeriFYD authority-like terms; an expanded reserved-name schedule must be published before new reservation rules go into effect.
- Availability is not an offer of immediate entitlement. A registration must pass backend uniqueness and any applicable sanctions, abuse and safety checks at the time of claim.

**Transition:** All current active, properly registered handles are grandfathered. A retroactively reserved or confusing name is reviewed individually rather than automatically taken from the incumbent. Never rewrite existing identity IDs, Trust Circle connections or messages during this process.

## 3. Who may register

**Individuals:** A person may register a lawful alias or pseudonym, without mandatory public disclosure of legal identity. Additional identity checks may be required only for specifically disclosed higher-risk privileges or recovery.

**Organizations:** Registration under a business or organization name requires separate, verifiable authority to act for that organization before receiving an organization-verified badge or delegated corporate control. Domain control alone does not necessarily establish the legal right to a business name or its trademark.

**Authorized delegates:** Future enterprise administrators may manage organizational handles only within explicit scopes, with logged delegation and revocation. An employee's personal handle remains separate from an employer's handle.

**Minors:** Do not enable directly discoverable registration or unsolicited external routing for under-13 users until a child-safety, consent, and privacy assessment is approved, including verifiable parental consent when applicable. Youth accounts must not be placed in a public searchable directory by default.

## 4. Rights of the registered holder

Subject to the currently applicable terms, privacy controls and law, the holder may:

1. Use their registered handle to identify themselves in VeriFYD-supported communication and profile features.
2. Maintain continuity of that allocation while the account remains eligible and in good standing.
3. Decide whether the handle is discoverable by the public, trusted connections or nobody, subject to a future user-access control design.
4. Control which supported calling, messaging, email and linked-service capabilities are reachable through the handle, subject to the underlying service's safety and regulatory constraints.
5. Review the status of the handle, request corrections, initiate secure recovery, report impersonation, and appeal adverse ownership decisions.
6. Export available account information under the applicable privacy and portability program; the exact scope of export must be disclosed.

These rights do not create an unconditional right to a particular unregistered string, a protected trademark, free paid services, unlimited aliases, a resale marketplace, third-party usernames, or uninterrupted network reachability.

## 5. Holder responsibilities

Registrants must use accurate information where identity or organization verification is specifically required; protect credentials; refrain from impersonation, harassment, fraud, trademark abuse, deceptive official status, malicious routing or unlawful use; respect contacts' block and privacy settings; and promptly report suspected compromise.

Use of a handle does not authorize unsolicited messages to everyone who can find it. Operator and third-party communication anti-abuse rules remain in force.

## 6. Reserved names and public-interest protection

The operator may reserve names needed for service operation, safety, government or emergency-service confusion prevention, official product and protected organization names. A public reserved-name policy should describe categories, not reveal security-sensitive individual names.

Do not grant a verification badge solely because a registrant selected a name like "official." Claims to government or emergency authority require strict checks, and unauthorized imitation can be refused. Registered marks are evidence for review, not an automatic entitlement to displace a pre-existing account.

Reserved-name changes must be recorded with reason, approving reviewer, policy version, timestamp and an appeal channel. Bulk hoarding, automated creation and speculative registrations are prohibited unless specifically permitted by a future, published business program.

## 7. Verified linked accounts and communications

A user may submit a social link, email route or phone contact only according to the feature's security controls. A mere user-entered profile URL is **not verified ownership** of an external social account. Ownership claims require the external provider's permitted proof method, such as OAuth authorization, documented delegated verification or an approved one-time challenge.

Public resolution must not publish underlying numbers, private mailbox addresses, location, verification documents, private Circle membership or unapproved social links. A generic mediation endpoint should be used where appropriate to protect private destinations.

Verification status should be explicit and limited, for example:
- "Email verified" — mailbox control verified under the service's procedure.
- "Organization verified" — organizational claim separately reviewed.
- "External account connected" — a secure link established, not proof of real-world identity.
- "Identity verified" — only if a dedicated process provides the stated level of assurance.

No verification indicator means global trustworthiness or immunity from fraud.

## 8. Changes, aliases and future transfers

**At initial release:** Handle renaming, handle sale, voluntary reassignment, multiple aliases, or export of an unqualified name to another provider are **not automatically enabled** by this Policy. Implement these only after technical, contractual and safety review.

If changing a handle is later offered, the future system must preserve the same internal identity ID, authenticate a high-risk request, log the previous allocation, provide a carefully defined cooling-off or quarantine period, and avoid forwarding private incoming communication to a different owner. A former handle's redirects should be opt-in, scoped and revocable, with privacy protections.

A future verified transfer between distinct owners requires a new explicit transfer policy, mutual authentication, fraud controls, a defined record of consent, review of encumbrances/disputes, and a clear explanation of whether old messages and trust links do **not** transfer. No speculative market or automatic resale right is created here.

## 9. Recovery and account compromise

Offer an authenticated recovery process independent of knowing the old password alone. Prefer existing identity/account sessions and MFA or passkeys when available, combined with risk-based verification for high-value or disputed accounts.

High-risk actions—including changing recovery contacts, verified business authority, the primary handle, or routing to new external destinations—should require step-up authentication and create an administrative audit event. Avoid exposing the current account's private contact methods while attempting recovery.

A suspected stolen account may be temporarily protected from transfers or route edits without removing the handle. Recovery decisions require documented evidence and an accessible manual appeal route. Do not require users with disabilities to complete an inaccessible recovery step.

## 10. Deletion, dormancy, suspension and reinstatement

An account deletion request must follow the applicable deletion process and law. Disable public discovery and unsafe new routing promptly. Preserve only those audit, fraud, dispute and legal records supported by a documented retention basis and delete or de-identify records at the end of that period.

**No automatic recycled-handle release policy is approved in this draft.** After deletion or long dormancy, hold a handle from reassignment pending an approved safety and privacy retention/recycling schedule. This protects prior contacts from messaging the wrong owner.

Suspension of communications privileges should be proportionate; whenever feasible preserve ownership records while addressing abuse. Immediate action is allowed for credible account takeover, active fraud, child safety threats, threats of violence, or legal requirements, subject to prompt review and a possible later correction.

## 11. Complaints and ownership conflicts

Permitted complaints include impersonation, fraudulent use of official or organizational status, verified business authority disputes, unauthorized transfer, account compromise, harassment, or evidence of unlawful misuse.

**Suggested process — subject to counsel and operator approval:**
1. Submission through an accessible, authenticated support form or designated external contact. Collect the minimum evidence needed and avoid posting victims' identity documents publicly.
2. Acknowledgement target: within 5 business days, unless immediate safety intervention is necessary.
3. Initial review target: within 15 business days for routine cases; notify parties of delay where lawful. Clearly separate emergency safety holds from final determinations.
4. Provide written issue, evidence categories, proposed action and a meaningful opportunity to respond before final reallocation when feasible.
5. Review conflicts against verified chain of control, current registration history, actual impersonation risk, prior rights, good-faith use, applicable law, and policy in effect at relevant times. Popularity or purchase price alone does not decide ownership.
6. Document outcome, approving reviewer, reasons, effective date, and applicable appeal instructions.
7. Proposed appeal period: 30 calendar days after final notice. Appeal by a different reviewer where practicable; cases involving an internal conflict of interest go to an independent reviewer.

These are **draft service targets, not currently operating guarantees**. The final policy must publish actual contacts, response commitments, escalation, and applicable legal rights before becoming effective.

## 12. Protection against arbitrary seizure

A handle should not be cancelled, reassigned or transferred solely because someone offers more money, becomes more famous, requests a preferred spelling, or holds a more expensive subscription.

Allowed actions require a lawful basis, a specific published rule, a substantiated ownership/safety finding, a voluntary authenticated request, or a legally binding order. Record the action and notify the affected holder where safe. A verification error by the operator should be corrected with proportional remediation and a documented appeal.

No unreviewed employee or administrator may use hidden privileged access to seize a handle. Administrative changes should have elevated authorization and independently reviewable audit trails.

## 13. Blocking, privacy and public discovery

The holder's public/private discovery choice is separate from their ownership allocation. A blocked party must not obtain a new ability to contact the holder merely by resolving the handle through an API or another app. Partner routing must honor relevant recipient trust, consent and block decisions.

Avoid exposing confirmation of an account's existence where doing so would facilitate stalking, enumeration, or harm. Lookup abuse controls should include rate limits, minimum-response disclosure and monitoring. **Being registered does not constitute consent to calls, direct messages, email marketing or profiling.**

## 14. Portability and third-party relationships

A VeriFYD-qualified identifier, such as acct:sample@vfvid.com, may be presented as an account identifier only when the domain-qualified discovery service is actually implemented. It does not establish control over @sample on other platforms or guarantee any independent provider will route calls or messages.

External portability or registrar support requires a published technical protocol, a receiving provider, user authorization, anti-hijack protections, revocation and conformance testing. No future feature may silently expose private routes or impose an irreversible association across otherwise separate identities.

## 15. Data handling, transparency and policy changes

Registry audits should retain only necessary facts: identity reference, handle/version, operation, authorized actor, reason category, immutable timestamp, and appeal outcome. Never write passwords, bearer tokens, recovery secrets, private therapy materials, or unredacted government documents to administrative logs.

The operator should publish aggregate transparency reporting on disputes, reversals, reserved names and enforcement while protecting identities and security-sensitive details.

Material changes affecting ownership, restrictions, fees, transfer rights, or public discoverability require clearly versioned publication, reasonable notice, relevant legal review, and required consent. A user who cannot accept a proposed incompatible term should be given the lawful options available for account continuation, data export and deletion—not a silently revoked handle.
